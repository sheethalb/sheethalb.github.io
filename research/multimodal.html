Research · structural MRI · multimodal learning

Learning from partly paired modalities

In a memory clinic, every patient gets an MRI, but only some get PET, a full clinical battery or a handwriting test. Can the extra data that exist for some patients make an MRI model better for all patients, without the model needing that data when it is used?

The setting

  • Main modality: T1-weighted structural MRI, used to tell Alzheimer's disease from cognitively normal ageing (ADNI).
  • Auxiliary modalities, each available for only part of the cohort: clinical scores, amyloid PET, and handwriting. The handwriting data come from a separate cohort that shares no subjects with the MRI cohort.
  • Used for training only. The auxiliary data act as privileged information. At test time the model sees MRI and nothing else.
  • Scanner effects are part of the question. ADNI mixes 1.5 T and 3 T scanners, so every model is evaluated separately per field-strength group.

Approach: prototype-anchored alignment

Questions under study

Which modalities help
Each auxiliary modality is added on its own and in combination, against an MRI-only model and a model trained only on fully paired subjects.
How much pairing is needed
The fraction of subjects with auxiliary data is varied to see how the method behaves as pairing becomes scarce.
Disease severity
Whether a model trained only on normal and Alzheimer's subjects places unseen mild cognitive impairment between them, in the right order, without ever seeing it during training.
Scanners and sites
Whether the learned representation is robust across field strengths and, in ongoing work, across independent cohorts (ADNI and OASIS-3 for training, with a held-out external cohort for testing).
Beyond the brain
Whether the same idea carries over to another domain: lung cancer pathology slides with gene expression (TCGA).

How it is evaluated

  • Five-fold cross-validation with several random seeds, scored as an ensemble on a fixed held-out test set.
  • Results reported per scanner field-strength group, not pooled.
  • Mild cognitive impairment subgroups are kept out of training and used only to test the ordering question.
  • In the multi-center work, each cohort's test set is scored separately and the external cohort stays untouched until the final evaluation.

This work is ongoing. A first abstract, "Exploiting Partially Overlapping Auxiliary Modalities for Alzheimer's MRI Classification via Prototype Alignment", appeared in the BAIOSPHERE Medical 2026 book of abstracts; full results will be added here once the papers are published.

Data: Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), OASIS-3 and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). This page describes aims and methods only.
