Network dynamics in Alzheimer's disease

Many studies report that brain network dynamics, and the default mode network in particular, change in Alzheimer's disease. Few test those claims together against motion and other confounds, the choice of brain atlas, the preprocessing pipeline, global signal and arousal, measurement reliability, and a second cohort. This study does that, with every analysis written down and locked before it is run.

Design

Cohorts and data

  • ADNI (discovery): cognitively normal, mild cognitive impairment and dementia groups from the clinical diagnosis at the scan; one resting-state scan per person, several sites and acquisition protocols.
  • OASIS-3 (confirmation): one centre; groups from the Clinical Dementia Rating at the nearest assessment (normal, very mild impairment, dementia).
  • Amyloid-defined groups from PET are used in a further analysis, so that "Alzheimer's" can mean biology and not only clinical diagnosis.
  • Both cohorts go through the same in-house preprocessing (described on the benchmark page), with the Schaefer-200 atlas and the seven Yeo networks; the default mode network is further split into anterior, posterior and temporal subregions.

What is measured

Phase dynamics

Metastability
How much the synchrony of brain regions varies over time: the variability of the Kuramoto order parameter of band-limited phase signals, for the whole brain, each network and each default-mode subregion.
Leave-one-network-out
How much one network contributes to whole-brain metastability, measured by recomputing it without that network.
Phase states
Recurring whole-brain phase-coupling patterns (leading-eigenvector dynamics analysis): how often each occurs and how long it lasts.
Intrinsic ignition
How widely activity spreads after a region's spontaneous events, and how variable that spread is, following the published reference implementation.

Connectivity and graph structure

Participation
Whether a default-mode region's connections stay inside its network or spread to others.
Efficiency and path length
How easily signals travel within the default mode network.
Anticorrelation
The strength of negative connections among default-mode regions.
Further measures
Hub disruption, salience-network coupling, modularity, rich-club organisation and hippocampus/amygdala coupling, each listed in the plan before being computed.

Clinical links

Associations with dementia severity, cognitive composites, amyloid and tau, and longitudinal decline, using cross-validated models for prediction.

Safeguards

Fixed primary measures
A small set of primary measures and regions is fixed in advance; anything added later is labelled exploratory, and a failed step is reported, not rescued.
Confounds
Nested models add age, sex, head motion, education, data retained after motion censoring, acquisition protocol or scanner, site, and brain atrophy.
Permutation tests
Group effects are tested by Freedman–Lane permutation within the covariate model, with effect sizes from the same model and false-discovery control within each family.
Size-matched nulls
Random sets of regions of the same size test whether a network is special or simply reflects how many regions it has.
Robustness
Global signal regression, an fMRI-based arousal estimate, physiological aliasing per protocol, five parcellations (Schaefer-200 and -400, Shen-268, AAL3, Dosenbach-160) plus group ICA and personalised parcellations, and an alternative preprocessing pipeline (DeepPrep).
Reliability and integrity
Split-half and test–retest reliability; unit tests on simulated oscillators; an independent second implementation for headline numbers; every reported number traced to its source file.
