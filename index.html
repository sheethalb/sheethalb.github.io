Dr.-Ing. · Research Associate · Junior Group Head of the LAMBDA colloquium (Learning Approaches for Medical Big Data Analysis)

I work on machine learning for medical imaging. My current focus is the brain: what resting-state fMRI can and cannot tell us about cognition in aging and Alzheimer's disease, how to learn from MRI when other modalities are only partly available, and how to evaluate such models so that the results hold up.

Before moving to neuroimaging I worked on chest X-ray and mammography AI: vision-language models, zero-shot classification and detection with few annotations, at Siemens Healthineers and the Pattern Recognition Lab. Earlier I spent several years in industry as an imaging systems architect at Intel and a software developer at IBM.

Neuroimaging and neurodegeneration

My current work asks what brain imaging can reliably tell us about cognition in ageing and Alzheimer's disease. With resting-state fMRI, I study how functional networks and their dynamics change across the disease, and test published claims under one shared, leakage-safe evaluation across cohorts (ADNI, OASIS-3, ABIDE) and preprocessing pipelines. With structural MRI, I develop models that learn from clinical scores, PET and other data available for only some patients, while needing only MRI at test time. A recurring question is reliability: whether a finding, or a per-person explanation, holds up on a repeat scan or a new cohort.

Vision-language models for medicine

Radiology images come with reports and health records that already describe what is in them. My doctoral work used this text as supervision: contrastive image-text models for chest X-rays that classify findings without task training (zero-shot), that localise findings at patch level (Patch-CLIP), and that cope with rare, long-tailed diseases and with biases between datasets. Related work tunes models to the operating point clinics actually use, such as high sensitivity at fixed high specificity (AUCReshaping). With students, the same ideas extend to prototype-based zero-shot classification and to retrieval-augmented diagnosis and report generation for retinal images.

Detection and localisation across modalities

Lesion detectors usually need many boxes drawn by experts for every new finding. I work on detectors that are guided by a few example images or by text instead: Exemplar Med-DETR for lesions in mammography, chest X-rays and angiography, and example-based grounding toward general-purpose detection models (EM-DETR). Student projects carry this to multi-view mammography, coronary stenosis, organ localisation and trauma detection in CT, and parameter-efficient segmentation in MRI.

Scan–Rescan Reliability of Individualized Network Attribution in Resting-State Functional MRI

Per-person explanations of which brain networks drive a connectivity-based cognition model are often proposed as biomarkers. This paper tests how reliable they are across repeat scans, against a permutation null that separates the person-specific part from the pattern everyone shares.
